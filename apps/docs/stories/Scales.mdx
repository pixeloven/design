# Scales

The non-colour primitives: radius, type, motion, shadow and stacking order.

Every value on this page is read from `packages/tokens/src/tokens.json` at build
time, and every shape is drawn with the token itself rather than a copy of its
number — so this page cannot show you something the package does not ship.

These scales are **scheme-independent**. A radius does not change with the
ground, so each carries one value rather than a dark and a light one. The
theme switcher in the toolbar changes the colours around them and nothing here.

## Where they came from

They were not invented for this package. All of them were promoted from
Lattice's `globals.css`, which was the only place they existed — reasoned out
there, in production, with the reasoning written down beside the numbers.

That is the same argument that made the palette a package, one layer up. A
colour system with no radius, type or motion scale is not yet a design system:
the second consumer hand-authors its own, the two drift, and nobody notices
until someone measures. It had already happened once here with colour.

## Radius

Squarer than the set it replaced. Four of five reference systems put a
**control** at 4px or below; a 10px radius on a 22px row curves through half the
row height and reads as consumer-soft.

`sm`/`base`/`lg` are 4/8/12 — the 4px grid, and 7px never can be. `xs` is 3px
and deliberately is *not* on the grid: on a 16px chip a 4px corner is already a
quarter of the height.

## Type

Seven steps, each carrying all four metrics a step actually needs. A step that
omitted its weight would not be a smaller defect than a wrong weight — the
consumer would silently inherit whatever the surrounding element had.

**Base is 13px, not 14px.** Mono at 13px sets 38 characters in a 320px panel
where 14px sets 35, and this family of products is overwhelmingly identifiers in
narrow columns.

**Line-heights are integer px, never unitless ratios.** A ratio produces
sub-pixel line boxes that break the 4px rhythm and make grouped lists drift down
the page.

**Tracking rises as size falls** — `-0.02em` at display, `+0.12em` at micro.
That is what makes small mono read as deliberate rather than cramped, and it is
asserted rather than left to taste.

<Scale group="type" />

## Easing

Two curves, and no more. No `ease-in` anywhere — starting slow reads as lag —
and no overshoot, which argues with anything physically simulated behind it.
Each curve below is drawn from its own control points.

<Scale group="ease" />

## Duration

**Duration scales with distance travelled, not with importance.** A hover fill
travels 0px; a tooltip 4px; a panel 320px. The bars are to scale.

Two pairs live here and they behave differently on purpose. `base`/`exit` is a
thing that enters the page: it eases in at 180ms and leaves at 100ms, because a
slow exit is the most common way an interface comes to feel sluggish.

`in`/`out` is **hover and focus**, which inverts that. Enter is 0ms — a hover
transition is feedback about your own pointer, whose position you already know,
and easing it in adds latency to the one interaction that must feel free. Exit
is 140ms because the pointer has gone and nothing waits on it, and the fade is
what stops a fast mouse crossing twenty rows from strobing.

<Scale group="duration" />

## Shadow

Reserved for popovers and above. A shadow on a resting card competes with the
elevation ramp, which is the real elevation signal in this system.

`lip` is the odd one and the most useful: a 1px inset line along a panel's *top*
edge. That is what the eye actually reads as "in front" — it works over a moving
canvas where a drop shadow does not, and it is derived from the text token's
`-rgb` companion rather than a literal, so it follows the scheme.

The alphas are tuned on the **dark** ground, because that is the only ground
with a production surface to derive them from. They are not wrong on light, but
they are heavier than a light scheme wants. Solving that means measuring a real
light surface rather than guessing, and guessing is what this package exists to
stop.

<Scale group="shadow" />

## Stacking

Four overlay families sit over the page, and an ad-hoc `z-10` sprinkled at each
call site is precisely how they end up fighting — a tooltip under a modal, a
toast behind a drawer. Steps of 10, so a surface can slot something between two
layers without renumbering the scale.

<Scale group="z" />

## Rules

1. **No literal radius, size, duration or z-index in a consumer.** The same rule
   as colour, and for the same reason.
2. **If no step fits, the scale is incomplete.** That is a contribution
   upstream, not a local override. A one-off `border-radius: 6px` is exactly how
   the colour copies drifted apart.
3. **Do not convert a scale locally.** Wanting rem instead of px, or a spacing
   ramp, is a change to this package — raise it here.
